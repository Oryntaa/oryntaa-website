Most software does not fail at launch. It fails six months later, when the person who
understood it has moved on, the tests never existed, and every change risks breaking
something no one can see. The Oryntaa standard exists to prevent exactly that.

## Senior engineers, on the actual work

The people you meet in the first call are the people who write the code. We do not sell a
senior team and staff a junior one. Founder-led means the person accountable for the
outcome is close enough to the work to feel it when a decision is wrong.

## A definition of "done" that includes the future

A feature is not finished when it demos. It is finished when it is tested, observable, and
documented well enough that a different engineer can change it safely next quarter. We
write the tests that matter, instrument the paths that carry real weight, and leave the
system easier to reason about than we found it.

## Decisions for the product you'll have in three years

Every architecture choice trades speed now against cost later. We are explicit about that
trade. Sometimes the right call is the quick one; often it is not. We make the call for the
product you are building toward, not the demo due next month — and we tell you which one
we are optimizing for and why.

## Shipping is the midpoint

The relationship does not end at launch. Production systems drift as usage and data change,
and the interesting problems usually arrive after real people start using the thing. We
build so that the second year is cheaper than the first, not more expensive.

That is the standard. It is not glamorous, and that is the point — it is what lets the work
keep paying off long after the launch announcement.
